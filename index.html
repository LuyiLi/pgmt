01 THE IDEA

General motion tracking,
beyond flat ground.

A motion reference tells the robot what to do. Perception tells it how to do it here.

PGMT turns terrain-agnostic motion commands into physically feasible whole-body behavior. By learning from independently selected motions and terrains, a single policy adapts its footholds, swing trajectories, and posture while preserving the intended motion.

Read the abstract +

Humanoid motion trackers can reproduce diverse whole-body motions, but their performance degrades on complex terrain where terrain-agnostic references become physically infeasible. We present PGMT, a Perceptive General Motion Tracking pipeline for humanoid robots that learns terrain adaptation from independently selected motion references and terrains. PGMT first learns a general tracking and recovery prior, then incorporates terrain perception through motion-conditioned terrain glimpses that selectively encode regions relevant to the current motion. Terrain-aware tracking relaxation allows necessary deviations from the reference while preserving its motion intent. Zero-shot deployment on a Unitree G1 demonstrates robust terrain-adaptive locomotion and whole-body motion execution over real-world terrain with obstacles up to 37 cm high, while supporting teleoperation, dynamic motion tracking, and fall recovery. PGMT extends general humanoid motion tracking beyond flat ground, providing a unified policy for terrain-adaptive locomotion, diverse whole-body behaviors, and teleoperation in complex environments.

03 THE METHOD

Learn to move.
Then learn where to step.

A general tracking prior, progressively extended
with motion-conditioned terrain perception.

STAGE 01

Build the motion prior

Learn diverse whole-body tracking and fall recovery on flat ground, using a motion-intent fusion module and a split-return critic.

STAGE 02

Inject terrain perception

Add local terrain glimpses, terrain-contact objectives, and tracking relaxation to adapt motion to complex terrain.

01 / PERCEIVE

Look where motion matters.

Four learned terrain glimpses focus on local geometry relevant to the current state and upcoming motion, instead of encoding every region equally.

02 / FUSE

Connect intent and terrain.

Cross-attention brings robot history, future motion references, and terrain tokens into a shared motion-intent representation.

03 / ADAPT

Preserve intent. Allow adaptation.

Terrain-aware relaxation permits necessary lower-body deviations while maintaining strict upper-body tracking objectives.

THE KEY DISTINCTION

Learn terrain adaptation inside the tracking policy, directly from unpaired motion and terrain data.
